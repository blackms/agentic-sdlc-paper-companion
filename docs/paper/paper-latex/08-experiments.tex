\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We report the eleven experimental phases in chronological order. Each phase has a pre-registered protocol (frozen before any reviewer call) and computational validation. Raw artefacts are committed at the paths indicated.

\subsection{Phase 1--3: pilot ($n = 120$ trajectories, 4 frontier models)}
\label{sec:phase1-3}

\textbf{Models}: OpenAI Codex \texttt{gpt-5.5}, Claude Sonnet 4.6, Claude Opus 4.7, Gemini 2.5 Flash. \textbf{Tasks}: T1 (compound-interest with overflow handling) and T2 (200 single-line injected bugs across $7$ categories in a finance domain). $n = 15$ trajectories per cell, $8$ cells. Conditions: free ($\protoZero$) vs constrained ($\proto$, with spec-first + TDD + asymmetric reviewer).

\textbf{Pilot finding} (negative): the Shannon-entropy formulation of $\Vmetric$ used in v0.1 of the framework returns $\eta < 0$ under uniform-failure collapse, because $H(\protoZero) < H(\proto)$ when both distributions concentrate on failure outcomes but $\proto$ has more variability in compliance components. Switched to expected utility $\E[u]$ plus eFOSD, validated in Phase 4.

\subsection{Phase 4 ($n = 120$): E1 validation, expected utility + eFOSD}
\label{sec:phase4}

\textbf{Result}: $\Delta\E[u] \in [+0.59, +0.78]$ across the $8$ cells; eFOSD verified on $80/80$ thresholds; $G \in [0.21, 1.11]$. Phase-4 also runs the $2 \times 2$ factorial (prompt-asymmetry $\times$ model-diversity) on $n = 200$ bugs $\times\, 7$ reviewer roles $= 1{,}400$ LLM reviews:

\subsection{Phase 5 ($n = 27$ + $n = 92$): L1 cross-domain falsification and C-T3 LLM-grounded falsification}
\label{sec:phase5}

\textbf{L1 cross-domain falsification.} The cold contract-first reviewer is re-deployed on \texttt{bankcheck.py} ($n = 27$ multi-line bugs across $10$ categories) but with \emph{finance contracts} (not domain-aligned). All $5$ pre-registered contrasts return paired McNemar $p \ge 0.5$. Cold reviewer detection collapses to $11\%$ on bankcheck. \textbf{Verdict:} the empirical mechanism of L1 is domain-bound; the mathematical bound continues to hold.

\textbf{C-T3 LLM-grounded falsification.} TRAIN/TEST split ($99 / 101$), top-5 categories injected via pattern names + 1-line descriptions, $n = 92$ paired. Result: $\Delta = -6.5\,\mathrm{pp}$, paired McNemar $p = 0.95$ against $H_1$ ``injected $>$ baseline''. \textbf{Verdict:} simulation-derived injection strategy does not improve LLM detection at the tested sample size.

\subsection{Phase 6: Cold reviewer recovery via auto-extracted contracts}
\label{sec:phase6}

To recover from the Phase-5 cross-domain collapse, contracts are \emph{auto-extracted} from the bankcheck reference module via a single Codex call. Cold reviewer detection rises from $11\%$ (misaligned) to $73\%$ (auto-aligned) on the same bug suite. Component-level transfer recovered.

\subsection{Phase 7: Second domain (JSON parser)}
\label{sec:phase7}

Cold reviewer with auto-extracted JSON-parser contracts vs cold reviewer with bankcheck (categorically mismatched) contracts: $n = 15$ bugs, paired McNemar $p = 0.0078$, $\Delta = +73\,\mathrm{pp}$. Bonferroni-significant. Recovery is robust to domain change.

\subsection{Phase 8: Three hand-written domains (multi-domain pilot)}
\label{sec:phase8}

Expression evaluator, regex compiler, HTTP header parser. Bug-injection yield $44\%$ (16/36 attempted via text-substitution), per-domain $n \in \{5, 6\}$, individually non-significant; Fisher's combined over P7 + P8 = $4$ tests: $p = 0.021$ (below $\alpha = 0.05$, but \emph{not} below pre-registered primary $p < 0.001$). Recorded as \emph{partial multi-domain generalization, underpowered}.

\subsection{Phase 11: Provenance-label experiment}
\label{sec:phase11}

Phase 11 ran in two passes. The v1.3 pilot used $30$ paired Codex calls per side ($60$ total); the v1.4 saturation replication extended this to all available AST mutations on the same two frozen reference modules: $53$ paired pairs for csv (\texttt{csv\_module.py}) and $60$ for chardet (\texttt{chardistribution\_module.py}). The v1.4 replication is pre-registered in \texttt{docs/paper/v1.4/preregistration-p11-replication.md} (frozen 2026-05-11 17:00) with Amendment A1 (\texttt{preregistration-p11-replication-amendment-A1.md}, frozen 2026-05-11 21:00) authorising a saturation variant after pre-flight enumeration established that $n=100$ per cell was infeasible on the frozen modules (csv: $53$ valid PRIORITY-1 mutations; chardet: $60$). Amendment A1 also relaxes the operator-mix-proportion match (BOR is exhausted in v1.3 on both modules); per-operator counts are reported in the manifest. The 1-line cover stories and the contract+prompt splices are byte-identical to v1.3 across all conditions; only the leading provenance statement changes between truthful and relabeled. Power for $\Delta=10\,\mathrm{pp}$ at $\alpha=0.025$ (one-sided Bonferroni-per-cell) under the saturation $n$ is $0.55$--$0.65$, an improvement over v1.3's $\sim 0.30$--$0.35$ but below the parent pre-reg's planned $0.80$.

At the pre-registered Bonferroni-per-cell $\alpha=0.025$, neither v1.4 cell rejects the null (csv McNemar $p=0.6128$; chardet $p=0.3125$). Bootstrap $95\%$ CIs on $\Delta$ are $[-13.2, +13.2]\,\mathrm{pp}$ for csv and $[-10.0, +3.3]\,\mathrm{pp}$ for chardet. \textbf{Decision rule: v1.3 conclusion stands at higher power.} The residual label-only effect is bounded by the v1.4 CI upper edges, i.e.\ at most $\sim 13\,\mathrm{pp}$ on csv (relabel-as-third-party direction) and $\sim 3\,\mathrm{pp}$ on chardet (relabel-as-stdlib direction), tighter than v1.3's $\pm 18\,\mathrm{pp}$. The same data broken into the $30$ v1.3-reused bugs and the new bugs gives consistent point estimates ($\Delta=0.0\,\mathrm{pp}$ on csv subgroups; chardet v1.3-reused $\Delta=-6.7\,\mathrm{pp}$, new $\Delta=0.0\,\mathrm{pp}$); see \texttt{experiments/p11\_replication/results.json}.

\textbf{Halt~\#6 (pre-registered)}: a v1.4 result that significantly reverses the v1.3 directional trend would force a §7.9 reframing. Neither cell is significant in v1.4 and the point estimates do not flip past zero on csv ($\Delta = 0.0$) or on chardet ($\Delta$ remains negative); halt~\#6 is \emph{not} triggered.

\textbf{Drift diagnostic}: the 30 v1.3-reused bugs were re-run truthful concurrently with v1.4 (separate output subdirectory). csv truthful detection rate moved from $43.3\%$ (v1.3, on disk) to $53.3\%$ (v1.4 re-run; McNemar $p=0.19$, NS) — a $+10\,\mathrm{pp}$ mid-strength Codex drift on csv. chardet rates were stable ($96.7\%$ both runs). The contemporaneous v1.4 main run on the same 30 reused csv bugs gave $60.0\%$ truthful $=$ relabeled — within the drift envelope. A KS two-sample test on per-bug truthful detection (v1.3-reused vs v1.4-new) reports $D=0.165$ and $p=0.835$ on csv (same-distribution sanity passes; the v1.4-new bugs are not measurably harder than the reused ones) and $D=0$ on chardet (identical means; the chardet baseline is saturated).

\textbf{Anti-stdlib carelessness as the dominant mechanism: not supported (at $n=53/60$ per side).} Label-only effects within roughly $\pm 13\,\mathrm{pp}$ on csv and $\pm 10\,\mathrm{pp}$ on chardet are below the saturation-$n$ power threshold and cannot be ruled out by this experiment; only the much larger ``label as dominant cause of the $\sim 53\,\mathrm{pp}$ sample gap'' interpretation is rejected. Operator-mix deviation under Amendment A1 (BOR exhausted) is documented; the same-distribution KS sanity is consistent with the deviation not introducing a measurable difficulty shift on csv. Methods, raw outputs, and the analyzer that produced these numbers are in \texttt{experiments/p11\_replication/} on branch \texttt{v1.4/exp-p11-rep}.

\subsection{Phase 12: Naturalistic bug benchmark on csv.py ($n = 8$)}
\label{sec:phase12}

\textbf{Motivation.} Phases 5--11 establish the C1 specificity finding on AST single-mutation bugs (AOR/ROR/BOR). Peer reviewers in successive rounds asked whether the contract-aligned advantage transfers to \emph{naturalistic} bugs harvested from real issue history. Phase 12 tests this on \texttt{Lib/csv.py}, the stdlib module with the lowest cold-detection rate in Phase 9 and therefore the most informative comparator. The pre-registration is at \path{docs/paper/v1.4/preregistration-extend.md} (frozen 2026-05-11).

\textbf{Harvest.} We enumerated all $80$ commits ever touching \texttt{Lib/csv.py}, applied the inclusion criteria (single-function-scoped, $\le 30$ LOC net change, no API change, behavioural fix, reversal patch applies cleanly to the v3.12 reference and produces an importable Python 3 source), and excluded documentation-only, refactor-only, perf-only, and Python-2-syntax-only fixes. Eight bugs survived (seven via direct \texttt{patch -R} on the merge commit, one via line-level manual port). This is below the pre-registered target of $25$--$30$; pre-registration risk \#1 anticipates this case and mandates ``proceed at $n$ harvested with reduced power''. Power at $n = 8$ for $\Delta = 30\,\mathrm{pp}$, $\alpha = 0.025$ paired one-sided is approximately $0.30$; the test is severely under-powered and the per-family significance read is qualitative.

\textbf{Method.} Three reviewer families (Codex \texttt{gpt-5.5}, Claude Opus 4.7, Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview) review each bugged source under \emph{cold} (auto-extracted \texttt{csv.py} contracts, byte-identical to the Phase 9 artefact) and \emph{cold\_mismatched} (the bankcheck contracts from Phase 6, byte-identical, deliberately wrong). Detection criterion is frozen at harvest: per bug, $5$--$9$ \texttt{expected\_detection\_keywords} (function name + bug summary terms + cited error type); a reviewer detects iff any keyword (case-insensitive) appears in \texttt{bugs\_found}. An out-of-band probe asks each reviewer whether they have seen this bug in the CPython issue tracker; cite-rate is reported as a leakage diagnostic and does \emph{not} enter the detection metric.

Per-family Bonferroni-corrected $\alpha = 0.025$. None of the three families crosses the threshold; Codex and Gemini sit just above it ($p \approx 0.03$). Under the looser family-wise envelope $\alpha = 0.075$, no family is significant either. \textbf{0/3 H1 replicates at the pre-registered threshold}, with the strong caveat that $n = 8$ paired permutations cannot reach $p < 0.025$ unless the entire effect is concentrated in one direction (here $5/8$ discordants for Codex and Gemini in the cold-wins direction, $0/8$ in the mm-wins direction).

\textbf{Leakage diagnostic.} CPython-issue-number cite-rate from the out-of-band probe: Codex $100.0\%$ (\textbf{above pre-registered $30\%$ caveat threshold}); Opus $0.0\%$; Gemini 3.1 Pro $12.5\%$. \textbf{Codex cites a CPython issue number on every bug:} its $75\%$ cold-detection rate cannot be cleanly attributed to contract reasoning---training-set memorization fully explains it. Per pre-reg decision rule, the Codex result is reported with strong caveat. Opus probes always answer ``NO''. Gemini cites $1/8 = 12.5\%$ (below threshold but non-zero).

\textbf{Naturalistic vs AST cold-rate on the same module} (Mann-Whitney U, unpaired because bug sets differ; secondary H1 predicts naturalistic $\le$ AST). Codex: $43.3\%$ AST vs $75.0\%$ naturalistic, $\Delta = -31.7\,\mathrm{pp}$, $p = 0.942$ (inversion aligns with the $100\%$ leakage signal). Opus: $30.0\%$ vs $12.5\%$, $\Delta = +17.5\,\mathrm{pp}$, $p = 0.162$ (direction as predicted, not significant). Gemini 3.1 Pro: $63.3\%$ vs $62.5\%$, $\Delta = +0.8\,\mathrm{pp}$, $p = 0.483$ (indistinguishable).

\textbf{Conclusion and interpretation.} At $\alpha = 0.025$, C1 specificity does \emph{not} replicate on naturalistic \texttt{csv.py} bugs in any of the three families ($0/3$). The Codex and Gemini point-estimates ($\Delta = +62.5\,\mathrm{pp}$) are large and in the predicted direction but cannot clear the Bonferroni threshold at $n = 8$ paired even when the effect is concentrated; the Codex signal is further confounded by $100\%$ training-set leakage of CPython issue numbers, and the Opus signal is consistent with no specificity transfer at this $n$. The secondary H1 (naturalistic $\le$ AST) is directionally supported only for Opus. The benchmark therefore reveals two residual concerns about the broader C1 result---small-$n$ underpower and leakage on widely-discussed stdlib code---and motivates a Phase-13 desideratum: a leakage-controlled naturalistic benchmark on post-cutoff or non-CPython libraries.

\subsection{Empirical summary}

Eighteen-plus cells across four phases (P7 + 3 P9 + 9 P10 + provenance) confirm the C1 specificity component. The absolute-detection moderator(s) are code-intrinsic and unidentified (Phase-12 desiderata: contract informativeness audit, matched-LOC stdlib/third-party experiments, operator-mix balancing, no-contract baseline).
