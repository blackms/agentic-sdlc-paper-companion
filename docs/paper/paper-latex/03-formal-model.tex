\section{Notation and Load-Bearing Primitives}
\label{sec:formal-model}

This section defines the minimal set of primitives used in \Cref{sec:decisional-metric,sec:asymmetric-verification,sec:learning-loop}. The full CMDP construction (state augmentation with regime modes, escalation map, mode-conditioned action sets) is published as a standalone technical report (\Cref{sec:cmdp-pointer}); it is an extension that this paper does not require for any empirical result.

\subsection{Agent as a stochastic transducer}

Let $x \in \mathcal{X}$ be the task specification and $\mathcal{Y}$ the model response space (token strings). The LLM is a parametric stochastic kernel
\begin{equation}
  \Mker(y_t \mid x, h_t), \qquad y_t \in \mathcal{Y},
  \label{eq:llm-kernel}
\end{equation}
where $h_t$ is the observable history $((a_1, o_1), \ldots, (a_{t-1}, o_{t-1}))$. A deterministic parser maps responses to actions: $\pi_{\mathrm{act}}: \mathcal{Y} \to \acts \cup \{\bot\}$. We treat $\Mker$ as a black box.

A trajectory is the sequence $\tau = ((a_1, o_1), \ldots, (a_T, o_T))$. The \emph{final artefact bag} $\artbag(\tau)$ is the multiset of files, test results, and decision-trace records produced during the run.

\subsection{Protocol}

A protocol is a tuple $\proto = (\acts, V)$ at the level of generality we need, where $\acts$ is the action set and $V = (V_1, \ldots, V_K)$ is a finite list of \emph{verifier kernels}. Each $V_k$ is a stochastic kernel
\begin{equation}
  V_k(v_k = \cdot \mid \tau_{1:t-1}, a_t, \mathrm{env}_t), \qquad v_k \in \{\mathrm{accept}, \mathrm{reject}, \mathrm{escalate}\}.
  \label{eq:verifier-kernel}
\end{equation}
The kernel form (i.e., $V_k$ is a random variable, not a function) accommodates noisy verifiers (LLM-based reviewers, flaky test runners). \textbf{This is load-bearing for \Cref{eq:p2-bound}}: the miss-indicators $M_i$ of \Cref{sec:asymmetric-verification} are well-defined as binary random variables only because $V_k$ is a kernel.

Verifiers split into protocol-internal $\VerC \subset V$ and held-out oracles $\VerS \subset V$. \emph{Free prompting} $\protoZero$ is the degenerate protocol with $\acts = \acts_{\mathrm{free}}$ and $V = \emptyset$.

\subsection{Held-out condition (information constraint)}

Let $Y_S(\tau)$ denote the random vector of $\VerS$ verdicts on $\tau$. The held-out condition is
\begin{equation}
  Y_S(\tau) \;\perp\; h_t \quad \text{for all } t \le T,
  \label{eq:holdout-condition}
\end{equation}
i.e., the held-out verdicts are conditionally independent of the agent's observable history given the task $x$. Operationally: no $\VerS$ verdict appears in any $o_t$ and no proxy of $\VerS$ (test outputs, traces, hints) appears in $o_t$ either. \textbf{This is load-bearing} for the $\PredC$ vs $\PredS$ distinction (\Cref{eq:predC,eq:predS}) and for ruling out oracle leakage as a failure mode (\Cref{sec:failure-modes}).

\subsection{Behavior signature, utility, and the conformance/semantics split}

The behavior signature is the deterministic projection
\begin{equation}
  B \;=\; \varphi(\tau) \;=\; \big(\, c_{\mathrm{form}}(\tau),\; c_{\mathrm{corr}}(\tau),\; c_{\mathrm{rob}}(\tau),\; c_{\mathrm{tok}}(\tau)\, \big),
  \label{eq:behavior-signature}
\end{equation}
with $c_{\mathrm{form}}(\tau) \in [0,1]$ the fraction of $\VerC$ verdicts accepted along $\tau$, $c_{\mathrm{corr}}(\tau) \in [0,1]$ the fraction of $\VerS$ held-out tests passed by $\artbag(\tau)$, $c_{\mathrm{rob}}(\tau) \in [0,1]$ the mutation-killing rate of agent-produced tests against a fixed mutant set, and $c_{\mathrm{tok}}(\tau) \in \N$ the total token cost.

The target utility is
\begin{equation}
  u(B) \;=\; \alpha\, c_{\mathrm{corr}} \;+\; \beta\, c_{\mathrm{rob}} \;+\; \gamma\, c_{\mathrm{form}}, \qquad \alpha + \beta + \gamma = 1, \quad \alpha + \beta > \gamma.
  \label{eq:utility}
\end{equation}
The choice $\alpha + \beta > \gamma$ ensures the utility is dominated by held-out components, avoiding the conformance-without-semantics trap.

The \emph{conformance / semantics split} is operationalized by
\begin{align}
  \PredC(\tau; \proto) &:= \ind\!\big[\text{all $\VerC$ verdicts on $\tau$ are \emph{accept}}\big], \label{eq:predC} \\
  \PredS(\tau; \proto) &:= \ind\!\big[\text{all $\VerS$ verdicts on $\artbag(\tau)$ are \emph{accept}}\big]. \label{eq:predS}
\end{align}
Two regimes are pathological: \emph{conformance without semantics} ($\E_\proto[\PredC] \gg \E_\proto[\PredS]$, quantified at $33$--$68\%$ in our $\proto$-condition data, see \Cref{sec:phase4}) and \emph{semantics without conformance} (rare, $\le 7\%$).

\subsection{Distributions induced by the protocol}

A protocol $\proto$, a fixed model $\Mker$, and a fixed task $x$ induce a distribution over trajectories and hence over signatures:
\begin{equation}
  \BehDist{\proto}{x} \;:=\; \text{law of } \varphi(\tau) \text{ under } \Mker, \pi_{\mathrm{act}}, V, \text{ given } x.
  \label{eq:protocol-distributions}
\end{equation}
We compare $\protoZero$ and $\proto$ by comparing $\BehDist{\protoZero}{x}$ and $\BehDist{\proto}{x}$ through $u$, stochastic dominance, and $\Vmetric$.

\begin{remark}[Notation: $V$ vs $\Vmetric$]
The symbol $V$ denotes verifier kernels (\Cref{eq:verifier-kernel}). The decisional cost-aware metric defined in \Cref{eq:decisional-metric} is denoted $\Vmetric$ (script V) to avoid notational collision.
\end{remark}
